### Gojko Adzic

**Source:** Gojko Adzic, *Impact Mapping: Making a Big Impact with Software Products and Projects*, Provoking Thoughts / Neuri (2012)

**Expert score:**
| Authority | Publication | Breadth | Adoption | Currency | Classification |
|-----------|-------------|---------|----------|----------|----------------|
| 4 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3 | **Established** |

Evidence (scoring-reports/expert-scoring-003.md in the AI Act Lab project, dual-pass verified, splits resolved down; full match): Authority — 2012 Jolt Award for *Specification by Example*; 2016 European Software Testing Outstanding Achievement Award. Breadth — acceptance testing and specification by example, product strategy, user stories, serverless. Adoption — *Specification by Example* widely listed among the most influential agile books of 2012; impact mapping used as a discovery technique in agile teams.

**Work score — *Impact Mapping*:**
| Specificity | Depth | Currency | Influence | Classification |
|-------------|-------|----------|-----------|----------------|
| 4 | 3 | 2 | 4 | **Recognised** |

Evidence: Specificity — a concrete four-level structure (goal, actor, impact, deliverable) with worked examples (split 5/4, lower recorded). Influence — the source of the technique the `/gvm-impact-map` artefact implements; translated, including Russian in 2022. Currency 2 (split 3/2): 2012, no later English edition. Boundary case: the primary pass averaged 3.75 (Established).

**Activation signals:** impact mapping, goal-to-deliverable traceability, scope control, discovery before requirements

**Key principles:**
- **Goal → Actor → Impact → Deliverable** — start from a measurable goal; name the people whose behaviour must change; state the change; only then name what to build.
- **Impacts are behaviour changes, not features** — "users escalate hard cases instead of overriding them" is an impact; "an escalation button" is a deliverable.
- **Deliverables earn their place by tracing up** — anything that does not connect to an impact and a goal is scope creep, however useful it looks.

### Marty Cagan

**Source:** Marty Cagan, *Inspired: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love* (2nd ed.), Wiley (2017). Later: *Empowered* (2020, with Chris Jones), *Transformed* (2024)

**Expert score:**
| Authority | Publication | Breadth | Adoption | Currency | Classification |

Evidence (scoring-reports/expert-scoring-003.md in the AI Act Lab project, dual-pass verified, splits resolved down; full match): Authority — founded Silicon Valley Product Group in 2001; former product leader at eBay, Netscape and HP. Publication — four books with Wiley. Breadth — product discovery, leadership, marketing, organisational transformation. Adoption — "empowered product teams" and "product operating model" are common industry vocabulary. Currency — *Transformed* (2024).

**Work score — *Inspired* (2nd ed.):**
| Specificity | Depth | Currency | Influence | Classification |
|-------------|-------|----------|-----------|----------------|
| 4 | 4 | 3 | 5 | **Established** |

Evidence: Specificity — concrete techniques for discovery, prototyping and testing. Depth — 368 pages, a complete rewrite of the first edition. Influence — a standard product-management text held widely in university libraries.

**Activation signals:** outcome-first goals, product discovery, value and feasibility risk, empowered teams

**Key principles:**
- **Outcomes over output** — a goal is a measurable change for users or the business, never a feature list.
- **Discover before you deliver** — test value, usability, feasibility and viability risks cheaply before building.
- **Small, fast experiments** — the cheapest prototype that answers the question beats a finished feature that answers it late.
